1 · Executive conclusion

KEEP MYLOOP. The replacement threshold (≈20% more useful work per wall-clock hour with equal or better correctness and recovery) is not met, and cannot be met by either candidate on this workload for three independent, measured reasons:

  1. The bottleneck is ticket supply, not orchestration. Over the 16 h window, 7 seats × 951 min = 6,657 seat-minutes. 44.6% went idle because no accepted ticket existed; 27.2% waited on a real ordering dependency or a human gate. Only 13.0% was a legal ticket waiting while a seat sat free (lead latency). Live board right now: 207 open rows, 200 without a ticket, 0 ready. A better dispatcher can recover at most the 13% slice; a better scheduler recovers nothing.
  2. Both candidates would have made correctness errors MyLoop did not make. Replaying the real dispatch epochs with each candidate's dependency semantics released 12 rows into an exclusive resource (the Docker stack or the warehouse) that another in-flight row was rebuilding, and Superharness's deps-only model additionally released one genuine same-file write conflict. Zero false-BLOCKED decisions were attributable to MyLoop's scheduler; every legal-but-undispatched row was the lead not acting, not the rule refusing.
  3. Neither candidate runs here as shipped. AIWorkHub refuses task creation unless its MCP server's parent is a VS Code-hosted Claude session (this machine's sessions report entrypoint: "cli") and its Windows worker launch is blocked by two localized bugs (a DLL export looked up in kernel32 instead of kernelbase; no windows_appcontainer branch in sandbox_argv). Superharness hard-codes bash and the util-linux script binary on its dispatch path; the escape is an undocumented-by-default env var plus Git Bash. Neither was executed against the backlog, so no wall-clock figure for either exists, and under the burden of proof that alone forbids REPLACE.

What the investigation did find is a short list of genuine MyLoop defects (non-atomic state writes that stop the whole loop on a torn file, three crash points that strand a row, no automatic LOST, a dispatch race) and two mechanisms worth borrowing from the candidates (version-checked updates and epoch-fenced claims). Those are one to two days of scripted work inside the existing design, listed in section 10.

3.2 Check misattribution: the wave problem in numbers

82 closed rows, 39 closed with an -Override (48%). Failed-check categories among those overrides: regression grader red on a changed tree 35, crosscheck 12, build/tests 8, repo changed under a mockup/doc row 6, grader 5, constraint 3. In 19 rows every failed check was attribution-class (another row's files, a lead-merged append line, a closed grader reacting to a wave-mate). Concrete cases: 52.S0.4y failed build/tests on 52.S0.8's half-landed edit and passed on re-collect; 52.S0.4v failed constraint on a census line the lead merged while it ran. This is the known 48.WAVE1 defect. It is a collect-design issue; a candidate scheduler does not see the compiler either (AIWorkHub isolates per worktree, which sidesteps it at the cost of graders that must run in a worktree, not against the live stack).

3.3 Candidate reliability, from their code and their own tests

AIWorkHub

Strongest recovery story of the three: durable reservation row before spawn, ledger replay classifying each request by PID identity (UNKNOWN defers, MISMATCH finalizes), epoch-fenced full-card CAS, reconciler thread every 30 s, Job Object kill-on-close. Chaos tests assert real things (crashed supervisor still routes to review; orphaned child is terminated; expired lease cannot mutate after reclaim). Weak spots: task row has no lease column; launch reservation lease is fixed 120 s and never renewed; no fsync on the ledger append; recovered work lands in blocked and needs a manager.

Superharness

State layer is genuinely solid (SQLite tx, version CAS, atomic claim, DB CHECK on status). Execution layer is weaker than the README: the only PID-aware inbox reclaim scans a status nothing writes, so worker death is noticed by 15 min log silence; a daemon killed between claim and spawn leaves a phantom launched row for 15–20 min; the orphan agent is never killed; daemon state file is a non-atomic write_text; Windows timeout terminates the direct child only (no tree kill). Its chaos suite covers DB concurrency and garbage files well, and one real runaway incident (max_retries reached 65) is now guarded.

7 · What we could delete (if a candidate were adopted)

Listed for completeness; the recommendation is not to adopt. Under Option A (Superharness as execution substrate): Claim-Lead/lead lock (replaced by watcher singleton), the OVERDUE half of Reconcile-Rows, Set-Pause, the Monitor fallback, the wave's LOST detection, dispatched.json/started.json bookkeeping (≈600 lines of PowerShell). Under Option D (AIWorkHub replaces all): additionally Get-ReadySet, Dispatch-Row's overlap and after checks, snapshots and constraint gate (its worktree + scope violation covers part), the collect class checks (its declared validation runs), the blind verifier prompt builder (its review packet). What would not be deletable under either: everything in section 8.

8 · What we must retain

  • Named exclusive resources and testProject: absent from both candidates; responsible for 12 of 13 shadow disagreements.
  • Ticket discipline: grader RED at dispatch, self-test with a trap stub, CLAUSE READING, the lead's clause read (4 of 6 reads found a defect no script could), unchanged-ticket refusal, two-strike rule, Residual gate at close. No candidate has an equivalent; Superharness's verify is caller-typed, AIWorkHub's validation is real but its "human" gate is an AI-supplied bool.
  • Live-tree execution: proofs run against the running Docker stack, the warehouse and the vault; worktree isolation is not a substitute, and "never commit, never branch" is a standing rule.
  • Regression graders at collect over closed rows whose files changed; the dispatch snapshot; append-only staging.
  • Board, schedule Artifact, TaskUpdate page in their fixed formats, regenerated on every state change.
  • Throughput instrumentation (events.jsonl → Get-Throughput) that made this evaluation possible in the first place.

9 · Migration architecture

Because the verdict is KEEP, the "proposed" flow is the current flow with four scripted additions (marked ★) and one storage change (marked ◆). Nothing moves out of .loop.

CURRENT

ticket → Test-Ticket → lead reads → lead runs Get-ReadySet, decides → Dispatch-Row (JSON in place) → Workflow wave (agents) → lead notices return, runs Collect-Row → lead runs verify wave, records verdicts → lead Close-Row / Reject → Sync-Board on wake crash: Reconcile prints; human -MarkLost

PROPOSED (Option C, in-house)

ticket → Test-Ticket → lead reads → ★ Auto-Dispatch: on every wake, dispatch the Get-ReadySet result (accepted tickets only) → Dispatch-Row ◆ atomic writes, per-attempt record, ordered multi-file commit + journal → Workflow wave (agents), unchanged → ★ Auto-Collect: Collect-Row -Json per returned row the moment the stage returns → verify wave, unchanged → lead Close-Row / Reject (judgment stays here) → ★ Sync-Board repairs: torn file → quarantine + rebuild from events; F1/F6 shapes → auto-fix ★ OVERDUE ≥ 3× + dead task ⇒ auto LOST + re-dispatch

10 · Migration plan: smallest reversible steps

Each step is a MyLoop row with a grader, a self-test and a blind verifier, like any other. Ordered by measured value ÷ risk.

  1. Atomic state writes (◆). Write-Json writes <file>.tmp.<pid> then File.Move with overwrite; readers of a torn file quarantine it to rows/_quarantine/ and continue. Removes F8/F9 (whole-loop stop). Test: truncate a row mid-write on a scratch copy, run Sync-Board, expect one WARNING and a normal OWED list.
  2. Dispatch journal + ordering rule. Dispatch-Row writes dispatched.json and the snapshot before the row record, and appends a dispatch-intent event first; Reconcile repairs a row with a record but no entry (F1) and a collected row with no entry (F6) from the journal. Test: F1 and F6 replayed, then Close succeeds.

13 · Method, confidence, caveats

  • Sources. MyLoop: every script under the skill (2,830 lines) and its 1,219-line lib read in full; 89 row records; 277 events; the board (436 rows, 207 open); MyLoopAnalysis.md; TaskUpdate.html (96 Queued, 5 Ticketed, 5 Started, 81 Complete at 09:43); the skill's own 18-test suite run on a scratch copy. Candidates: shallow clones at the stated heads; one fresh-context inspector each, 19 capabilities, file:line evidence; the lead re-read the eleven claims the verdict rests on. Both candidate test suites were executed here (results in section 2.2).
  • Not done, and why. No candidate executed the backlog: AIWorkHub cannot create or launch on this host as shipped; Superharness would need Git Bash on PATH, an env escape and an untested launcher path, and the user's standing rules forbid detached agents running with permissions bypassed against the live repos. So no candidate throughput, utilization or token figure exists. The scorecard gives them 0 in those two categories rather than an estimate.
  • Replay limits. Ticket readiness before 09-15 17:54 is not in the event log, so the window is 16 h and 39 closed rows. Rows dispatched without a ticket-check event are treated as ready at dispatch (conservative: no lead latency charged). The "real dependency block" bucket includes gates waiting on their own unticketed input, which is partly supply in disguise. Candidate semantics are re-implementations from their code, not their code.
  • Crash injection limits. Faults were planted at six chosen points plus torn files and a race; a real power cut can hit any byte. The scratch copies carried the live .loop as of 09:34, with status-page writes disabled.
  • Confidence 0.85 in KEEP. The three legs (supply-bound seats, candidate false READY on exclusive resources, candidates not runnable here) are each independently measured; any one alone would forbid REPLACE under the stated threshold. Confidence in the exact percentages is lower (±5 points; one-window sample). Confidence that steps 1–3 of section 10 remove the stranding shapes is 0.9 (they are the standard remedies and the failures reproduce deterministically).

Final answer to the closing question. Same backlog, same machines, same Claude Code capacity, same models, same safety constraints: no, neither alternative finishes sooner or with fewer orchestration failures. AIWorkHub does not start; Superharness would release rows into a shared stack that MyLoop correctly holds, and would notice a dead worker later than the wave does. The partial answer is internal: atomic writes, a dispatch journal, auto-collect and auto-dispatch inside MyLoop, worth at most a 13% seat-capacity recovery and the removal of every stranding shape measured here.

14.2 · Which concepts do not transfer, and why

  • One scalar. Autoresearch minimises one number under one fixed budget. A loop has hard invariants (a false READY is not a slower loop, it is a wrong one) and several throughput figures that trade against each other. The answer is a lexicographic order with a hard-constraint gate on top, not a weighted sum.
  • "The only constraint is that the code runs without crashing" (program.md:33). In a training run, not-crashing is a near complete proxy for validity. In a loop the dangerous failures do not crash: a lost lease, a double claim and a stranded row all complete "successfully" and may raise throughput. Validity must be a positive invariant check, or the optimiser learns to delete the safety.
  • A stateless, repeatable run and a keep rule finer than the noise. The source discarded a seed change as a regression and kept deltas smaller than that. Only the scheduling layers are replayable here (deterministic, zero noise); anything an agent sees (author prompt, model routing, compression) must be measured live, paired, on matched rows, with a pre-registered decision rule and a minimum n — which is what the 09-15 Fable-vs-opus measurements did by hand.
  • Fixed wall-clock training time as the equaliser. The MyLoop equivalent is a fixed WORKLOAD (same rows, dependencies, durations, outcomes), not a fixed time: two candidates must see identical work, then time or completed work is measured.
  • The agent edits the live file and commits. The production skill folder is live under a running loop, commits are forbidden to agents by standing rule, and promotion is a reviewed row.
  • Prose-only anti-cheat. With a scalar loss the incentive to tamper is low; when the objective is throughput and the constraints are invariants, "make it faster" and "weaken the check" are adjacent edits. This is the design choice replaced first: hashes, a runner that refuses out-of-surface paths, and the evaluator re-run from a pristine copy.

  • the evaluator (Measure-Fitness.ps1), the hard-constraint list, the KEEP rule, the baseline records, the ledger's past entries;

14.5 · The fitness function

Two experiment classes, because two kinds of evidence exist.

Class A — replay experiments (scheduler, dispatch, collect, reconcile, knobs). Deterministic and cheap: the evaluator drives the REAL scripts against a scratch .loop with a simulated clock and recorded workers (a worker "returns" its recorded outcome after its recorded duration).

Class B — live paired experiments (authors, models, prompts, compression). Two arms on matched rows (same class, same clause-count band), a pre-registration file written before the first row runs, minimum n = 6 pairs, both arms in the same day.

Lexicographic; a candidate is compared with best-known at the first level where they differ:

LevelMeasureRule
L0 correctness (hard)false_READY (against the shadow oracle), invalid_CLOSE (closed without the class's checks, verdict or residual), lost_work (a key that vanished, an attempt with no terminal state), duplicate_execution (two live attempts of one key), state_corruption (an unparseable authoritative file), unrecoverable_crash (a RECOVERY-REQUIRES-HUMAN the baseline did not produce), stale_epoch_write, dependency_violation, exclusive_collision, verification_bypass (a code or security row with no verdict; a check list shorter than the class's)every count ZERO, else REJECT
L1 reliabilityreconcile repairs, manual interventions, retries per closed row, chaos-suite pass countnone worse than best-known
L2 useful throughputClass A: est-minutes of CORRECTLY closed rows per simulated wall hour over the whole workload (an incomplete workload = REJECT); legal-seat fill (Σ in flight ÷ Σ legal); READY→dispatch and DONE→collect latency. Class B: correct completed rows per wall hour on the paired rows; for authors, ACCEPTED tickets per hour weighted by downstream worker success (a ticket whose row is later rejected counts 0)the primary must improve by a margin: A ≥ 2 % (replay noise is zero; the margin stops churn); B: paired median ≥ 15 % with a sign test at p ≤ 0.1
L3 token efficiencytokens per correct closed row; tokens per accepted successful ticketnot worse by more than 10 % unless L2 improved by more
L4 operational simplicitylines changed, knobs added, files addedtie-breaker only, and only outside the invariant surface

Why est-minutes and not row count: the estimate is frozen in the workload, so a candidate cannot earn by preferring short rows; a row counts only when the evaluator observed it closed with every check its class requires. Why lexicographic and not weights: no measured exchange rate exists between a false READY and a minute of latency, and inventing one is how a scheduler learns to be fast and wrong. Why a margin on a deterministic replay: the source's ledger shows what a zero-tolerance rule does — kept deltas below the noise floor and a seed change logged as a regression.

14.7 · Can the existing replay + crash + chaos infrastructure serve as the evaluator?

Partly today, fully after 48.R11. What exists: the shadow-replay script from the verdict session (27 epochs, MyLoop semantics as oracle; it lives in that session's scratchpad and must be rescued into the vault beside the verdict), Inject-Faults.ps1 (ten crash points, scratch copies), Test-MyFableNext.ps1 (18 cases), Get-Throughput.ps1 (every latency in section 5). What each lacks for evaluator duty: the replay simulates the scheduler and does not drive the real scripts; fault injection is by hand; the throughput report reads live events, not a simulated run.

14.8 · Missing benchmark infrastructure

  1. Frozen workload bundle with a manifest hash: one script that cuts it from a live .loop at a chosen window.
  2. Loop simulator: drives the real scripts on a scratch .loop with a fake clock and recorded workers — the largest piece, and shared with 48.R11 (crash labels at "after worker DONE" need it).
  3. Measure-Fitness.ps1: runs L0–L4, writes the experiment artifact, decides KEEP/DISCARD.
  4. Sandbox runner: copies best-known to exp-<id>\, applies the candidate, verifies the immutable hashes, runs the evaluator, refuses any path under the production skill folder.
  5. Ledger: experiments.jsonl append-only plus one artifact folder per experiment, and a Find-Experiment query the researcher must run before proposing (the source's ledger is written and never read; its multi-agent form makes reading it step 1).
  6. Paired live protocol for Class B: row matching, pre-registration file, the sign test.

14.9 · KEEP vs DISCARD

KEEP iff every L0 count is 0 AND no L1 figure is worse than best-known AND the L2 primary beats best-known by its margin AND L3 is within tolerance AND the immutable hashes match. Otherwise DISCARD, naming the first failing level. Both outcomes write the same artifact: id, parent baseline id, hypothesis (one sentence), files changed (diff hash), expected improvement, correctness result, crash result, chaos result, replay result, performance result, token result, decision, reason. The artifact's intent half (id, parent, hypothesis, expected improvement, diff hash) is written BEFORE the run — the commit-before-run idea — so a researcher that dies mid-experiment leaves a labelled state.

14.10 · Persisting best-known versions

Not git commits: agents never commit in this environment, and the skill folder is untracked on a share. Folder copies with a manifest: Loops\Autoresearch\best-known\ (scripts, foreman.json, a hash manifest, the artifact that made it best); Loops\Autoresearch\exp-<id>\ per experiment, kept, never deleted (a DISCARD keeps its diff so the ledger can show what was tried). Best-known advances only by copying a KEEP experiment's folder over it and appending to the ledger. Every experiment starts from best-known, never from another experiment. The source's frontier-as-DAG (agenthub) is the right shape if two researchers ever run at once; one branch suffices for one.

14.11 · Promotion to production

A KEEP experiment becomes a MyLoop ROW: ticket with the hypothesis as WHY, the diff as the write set, a grader that re-asserts the evaluator's L0/L1 results against the LIVE scripts plus a self-test, the staging-copy landing, the blind verifier, the lead's read, the close. Nothing in the sandbox can write to the production skill folder; the runner refuses a path under it.

14.12 · Who runs the experiments

Evidence on record (MyLoopAnalysis §3, pre-registered on 09-15): Fable as verifier, −12 to −14 % tokens, faster, more findings; Fable as ticket author, +21 % tokens, −18 % wall, one ticket undelivered; opus everywhere by default since 09-13. The division that follows: Fable proposes the hypothesis (reads the ledger and the throughput report; low volume, high judgement), interprets the artifact, and reviews a promotion ticket. Opus implements the bounded diff in the sandbox — the author-shaped seat where Fable measured worse. No model runs the evaluator: it is a script. A cheaper model has no measured seat here yet. This is a hypothesis to test on the first ten experiments, not a setting.

14.13 · Cost

Build: six rows after 48.R11 — workload bundle (1), simulator (2, the largest), fitness + ledger (1), sandbox runner (1), researcher skill (1); about one and a half worker-days at the loop's measured 0.3–0.5× of estimate. Per experiment: Class A about 15 min wall and 250–400k tokens (a Fable read plus an opus diff); Class B a day of paired rows and their real tokens. The source runs 12 experiments an hour on one GPU; Class A here would run about 4 an hour, bounded by the chaos suite.

14.14 · Largest risks

Simulator fidelity (a scheduler that wins on recorded durations may lose on live ones — mitigated by promoting only through a real row); overfitting to one frozen workload (rotate bundles; a KEEP must hold on two); evaluator drift (the immutable set is hashed, and a change to it is itself a reviewed row); token burn on Class B; a researcher that edits production by path mistake (the runner refuses); and the temptation to widen the mutable surface into dependency semantics because that is where 27 % of blocked seats sit — that surface stays closed.

14.15 · First five experiments

  1. (A) Seat refill: dispatch the legal set immediately after Collect-Wave returns vs on the next wake only. Expect READY→dispatch latency to fall; measures the 13 % lead-latency slice directly.
  2. (A) ReadySet packing order: board order vs longest-after-chain-first vs shortest-est backfill. Expect higher legal-seat fill with zero L0 change (legality untouched).
  3. (B) Ticket authors 3 vs 4 concurrent at prefetch depth 6: accepted-and-successful tickets per hour, tokens per accepted ticket, lead-read minutes. The 45 % bucket.
  4. (B) Verifier routing: Fable vs opus on security rows only. Tokens, wall, findings that survive the lead's read.
  5. (B) Author evidence pack: full file:line pack (today) vs pointer-only. Tokens per accepted ticket and rejection rate.

Then, sixth: (B) worker-shell output compression (RTK) measured on total completed-work economics, never on shell bytes.

FINAL VERDICT

BUILD AFTER RELIABILITY HARDENING

Evidence: the evaluator's hard-constraint layer IS the hardening programme's proof (48.R11's crash and chaos suites, the reconcile that makes stranded state deterministic); without it a candidate's stranding cannot be told from the baseline's, and the baseline's own reliability figures are non-zero today (4 hand-marked LOST attempts, 42 of 84 override closes). The orchestrator-addressable throughput slices (13 % lead latency, the collect wait) are being closed deterministically by 48.R8/48.R9 without experimentation. What remains — ticket supply at 45 % of seat capacity, model routing, prompt shape — is the Class B territory where a controlled, ledgered, paired protocol beats occasional hand measurement: the 09-15 Fable runs produced one real win and one real loss in the same evening, which only a repeatable process would catch every week. The source proves the loop shape works (126 experiments, 23 kept, unattended); its own ledger also proves the two things that must change for this domain — a keep rule wider than the noise, and an evaluator the agent cannot edit.
